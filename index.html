For your home Wi-Fi

See who’s on your home Wi-Fi

Perimeter keeps an eye on your home network: who joins, who leaves, and anything on your router worth fixing. It tells you in plain words.

It runs on a Mac that stays at home. Your iPhone and your web browser show what it sees. Everything stays on your Mac.

Download for Mac Download on theApp Store

Free for home use. No account. For Macs with an Apple chip, on macOS 14 or later; iPhones on iOS 17 or later.

What it shows you 05

Three screens say the same things in the same words: the iPhone app, the Mac’s menu bar and a page in your web browser. Here they are, on a made-up household.

The checkup

Is anything wrong?

The top of the screen answers that in one line: “All clear”, or the one thing worth a look.

Under it, short notes say what each thing is, why it matters and what to do, a step at a time, in the words your router’s own settings page uses. Each says how much it matters in words as well as colour: fix soon, worth a look, or good to know.

The devices

Who’s on your Wi-Fi?

Every phone, laptop, TV, speaker, doorbell and bulb, in groups you recognise, under the names you gave them.

A device Perimeter has never seen is the first thing you read about, with two answers: “It’s mine”, or “I don’t know it”. If nobody recognises it, Perimeter gives you a short plan.

A note

What to do about it, one step at a time

Every note says what is normal before what is worrying, and puts the plain words before any technical one. A button opens your router’s own settings page.

Anything you are happy to live with, you put aside with “Got it”. It moves to the checks that passed, and comes back only if something changes.

A device

Each one, and anything about it

Open a device to see what it probably is, when it first joined, and any note that points at it.

Give it a name and it keeps that name, whatever it calls itself. Here is a router, with the note about its push-button pairing (WPS).

The week

Your week, an hour at a time

Run a finger along the week at the top of the checkup and the whole screen goes back to that hour: who was here, and who had not arrived yet.

Each device ends with its own week, a square for every hour. An hour when nothing could look, because the Mac was asleep, is marked as that, never as “away”.

On your Mac

The Mac app puts a small mark in the menu bar, with a number beside it when something needs a look, and a panel under it. Choose “Dashboard” and the same two screens open in your web browser: the checkup on the left, the devices on the right.

How it works 05

Five steps. None of them needs anything technical.

  1. 01

    Put it on a Mac that stays at home

    Download Perimeter for Mac, open the file and drag Perimeter into your Applications folder. Open it: a small mark appears in your menu bar. Choose “Set up Perimeter” once. There is nothing to type and nothing else to install.

  2. 02

    Say which network is yours

    Choose “Watch this network”. Perimeter shows you the network it is on and asks first. It looks at nothing you have not chosen, so a laptop taken to a café does nothing there.

  3. 03

    Give it a day to learn

    For the first 24 hours Perimeter learns what normally lives on your Wi-Fi. Phones wander home all evening, and none of them is news. After that, a device it has never seen is the first thing you read about.

  4. 04

    Read the checkup

    Every five minutes it looks again and writes a short list. The top line answers “is anything wrong?”. Under it are notes, each saying what it is, why it matters and what to do. It tells you, once, when something needs a look.

  5. 05

    Add your iPhone, if you like

    In the menu bar, choose “Pair iPhone” and point your iPhone’s Camera at the code. From then on your iPhone talks straight to your Mac over your home Wi-Fi. The Mac does the checking; the iPhone shows what the Mac sees, so it has news while the Mac is at home and awake.

What it checks 07

What a careful neighbour would look at, and then say what to fix.

Doors to the internet
Games, video calls and file-sharing apps ask your router to let the internet in, and so does malware. Perimeter asks the router what is open now, and says which doors belong to an app you would know.
Push-button pairing
Push-button pairing (WPS) lets someone in range guess their way to your Wi-Fi password. Perimeter tells you when your router has it on.
Wi-Fi protection
Whether your Wi-Fi is open, or protected in an old way that can be broken.
Risky doors on devices
Old-style login, file transfer, remote desktop. About once a day each device on your own network gets a single knock: a connection opened and closed at once. Nothing is sent, and no password is tried.
New and quiet devices
A device Perimeter has never seen, and the ones meant to be on all the time, like a camera or a printer, when they stop answering.
Your router
When it changes, or when something else on your network answers as if it were the router.
Gadgets
Four or more smart bulbs, plugs, cameras and TVs on the same network as your computers, and a suggestion to give them a guest network.

Private by design

Everything Perimeter learns about your network stays on your Mac. There is no account, no sign-in, no tracking, no analytics and no adverts, and nothing is sent to us.

The one thing it can fetch from the internet is a public list of hardware makers, and only when you ask. Your iPhone talks straight to your Mac over your home Wi-Fi, encrypted. Nothing goes through the internet, or through anyone else’s servers.

What it does not do

  • It never looks at a network you have not told it to watch.
  • It never changes your router. It tells you where to click.
  • It never logs in to anything.
  • It never sends a hardware address (MAC) anywhere, not even to your iPhone.
