Help with Perimeter

The questions people ask first, in plain words. If yours is not here, write to us.

  1. 01

    What do I need?

    A Mac that stays at home, and ideally stays awake. Perimeter does its checking there. It needs a Mac with an Apple chip (M1 or later) and macOS 14 or later.

    Not sure which you have? Choose the Apple menu, then “About This Mac”. A Mac with an Apple chip says “Chip” beside the name of the chip; an older one says “Processor”.

    An iPhone is optional. It needs iOS 17 or later, and shows what your Mac sees. There is no account to make and nothing to type.

  2. 02

    How do I pair my iPhone?

    Pairing tells your iPhone which Mac to read. You do it once. Both need to be on your home Wi-Fi.

    1. On your Mac, open Perimeter in the menu bar.
    2. Choose “Pair iPhone”.
    3. Open the Camera on your iPhone, point it at the code, and tap the Perimeter banner.

    The first time, your iPhone asks whether Perimeter may find devices on your local network. Choose Allow: it needs that to reach your Mac. If the code will not scan, choose “Copy link” on the Mac, then “Paste” in the iPhone app.

    Keep the code to yourself. Anyone who scans it can see your devices.

  3. 03

    It says it can’t reach my Mac

    Your iPhone reads what your Mac saw, so it only has news while the Mac is at home, awake and on the same Wi-Fi. Until then it shows the last answer, and says how old it is. Nothing is lost, and nothing is wrong yet.

    What to try

    1. Check your iPhone is on your home Wi-Fi. If it says “Your iPhone isn’t on Wi-Fi, so it can’t reach your Mac”, that is the reason: away from home, it cannot reach your Mac.
    2. Check your Mac is on, at home and awake. Wake it, wait a minute, and try again.
    3. Look for Perimeter’s mark in your Mac’s menu bar. If its panel says “Perimeter has stopped running on your Mac”, choose “Start it”.
    4. If the iPhone says “Perimeter needs Local Network access to reach your Mac”, open Settings on the iPhone, choose Privacy & Security, then Local Network, and turn on Perimeter.
    5. If it says “This iPhone isn’t paired with your Mac any more”, choose “Pair again” and pair it once more.

    If your router gave your Mac a new address, the iPhone finds it again by itself. Give it a minute. If none of this helps, write to us and tell us what the screen says.

  4. 04

    My Mac was asleep

    Perimeter looks every five minutes, and only while your Mac is awake. When the Mac sleeps, nothing is checked, and the screens say so in words: “Your Mac was asleep from Sat 22:14 to 07:30 today, so nothing was checked then. Checking again now.” It looks again the moment your Mac wakes.

    • A device that could not be checked while the Mac slept is shown as “not checked”, never as “away”.
    • No notification is sent on the strength of a check the Mac slept through.
    • It says “Perimeter stopped checking” only when your Mac has been awake for a while and nothing was checked.
    • If your Mac sleeps through most of the week, a “good to know” note says Perimeter only watched while your Mac was awake.

    Want it to watch through the night? Keep the Mac plugged in, and in System Settings look for the option that stops it sleeping automatically while it is plugged in. Nothing breaks if you do not: Perimeter is honest about the hours it could not look.

  5. 05

    A device I don’t recognise

    Most of the time it is someone at home setting up something new. Perimeter tells you once, and only after it has seen the device in two checks, so a phone that has only changed its address has a chance to be recognised first.

    Phones, tablets and laptops often show as “A phone or laptop”. They hide their maker behind a private Wi-Fi address, to protect their owners. That is normal.

    What to do

    1. Open the note. It says when the device first joined and what it probably is.
    2. Ask the people you live with, and any guests, whether they have just joined.
    3. If someone does recognise it, choose “It’s mine”. You can give it a name with “Give it a name”, so you know it next time.
    4. If nobody does, choose “I don’t know it”. Perimeter turns the note into a short plan: look at the maker, and if it is still a mystery, change your Wi-Fi password, so that every device has to join again. Then see who comes back.
    5. Perimeter keeps watching it, and tells you when it leaves or comes back. When you find out whose it is, choose “It’s mine” after all.

    A note that says a device is answering as your router is more serious: it is marked “Fix soon”. Follow the steps in it.

  6. 06

    What does it not do?

    Perimeter tells you what it sees, and what to do about it. It does not do things for you.

    It never

    • looks at a network you have not told it to watch. A laptop taken to a café does nothing there.
    • changes your router. It reads what the router volunteers and tells you where to click. It never opens or closes a door itself.
    • blocks, switches off or removes a device. It cannot make anyone leave your Wi-Fi.
    • logs in to anything, or tries a password. A knock on a device’s door opens and closes a connection, and that is all.
    • sends anything about you to the internet. The one download is a public list of hardware makers, and only when you ask. The privacy policy says exactly what leaves your Mac.
    • sends a hardware address (MAC) anywhere, not even to your iPhone.
    • says “nothing changed” about time it was not looking, or “nothing there” about a check it could not make.

    What it cannot see

    • “All clear” means nothing Perimeter knows how to check is wrong. It does not look inside what your devices send, and it cannot tell you whether a device has been hacked.
    • It only watches while your Mac is at home and awake.
    • A phone that changes its private address and never announces a name, as many Android phones do not, can look new after it changes.
    • It lists the doors that apps opened by asking your router. A door set up by hand under “Port forwarding” is not on that list. A router with its automatic door-opening (UPnP) turned off keeps no list, so Perimeter says nothing about doors rather than “no doors”.
    • It reads how your Mac joined the Wi-Fi, so it says nothing about the router’s other networks, such as a guest network, or about a cable.
    • It runs on a Mac with an Apple chip. There is no version for Windows or Android, and the iPhone app only shows what your Mac sees.
  7. 07

    How do I remove it?

    Everything is on your own devices, so you can remove it all yourself. Choosing Quit in Perimeter’s panel only closes the Mac app: the checks keep going in the background until you do the steps below.

    On your iPhone

    1. Open Perimeter, open its settings and choose “Unpair this iPhone”. That removes the pairing and the last answer.
    2. Delete the app, as you would any app.

    On your Mac

    1. In Perimeter’s panel in the menu bar, choose “Pair iPhone”, then “Turn off phone access”, then choose Quit.
    2. In Finder, choose Go, then “Go to Folder…”, type ~/Library/LaunchAgents and press Return. Drag the two files whose names start with dev.perimeter to the Bin.
    3. Restart your Mac, so that the checks stop.
    4. In Finder, choose Go, then “Go to Folder…”, type ~/.perimeter and press Return. Drag the folder it opens, with everything Perimeter learned, to the Bin. If you cannot find it, go one step up to your home folder and press Command, Shift and full stop to show hidden folders.
    5. Drag Perimeter from your Applications folder to the Bin, then empty the Bin.

    The Mac app also leaves a tiny settings file about itself in your Library folder. It holds nothing about your network.
