What’s new

What changed

What changed for the person using Perimeter, version by version.

Version 1.0 · 12 October 2026

A Mac that sleeps, finished

0.4 stopped blaming the devices for the hours your Mac slept. These are the places it still did.

  • A device the last check couldn’t look for is “not checked”, never “away”. That is when your Mac fell asleep in the middle of that check, or when part of Perimeter couldn’t see. The group already said so; now the count at the top of the devices says it too, as in “27 here now, 3 away, 1 not checked”.
  • A device first heard in a check your Mac slept in gets its first-seen square in its week, and its week says the hour it was first seen.
  • A device that stopped answering is quiet since the first check that looked for it, not since a check that began the evening before and finished in the morning.
  • A new device is announced even when another check running at the same time was one your Mac slept in.

If you use the Terminal

  • The Terminal’s list of devices says “not checked” too.
  • “Back after 9 days away”, in the written report, counts only the checks that looked for the device. A holiday your Mac slept through is not nine days away, and a check the Mac slept in is never why a device is back.
  • The report’s blind spots say when your Mac was asleep, as in “No checks Sun 23:00 → Mon 07:05: your Mac was asleep”, and when the last check is hours old because the Mac went to sleep since.

Behind the scenes

  • Every change is now written down in a changelog.
  • The tests run on macOS and Linux, with every version of Python that Perimeter supports, 3.11 to 3.14.

Version 0.4 · 6 October 2026

The checkup

0.2 and 0.3 said who was on the network and when. 0.4 answers what someone who is not technical asks: is anything wrong, and what do I do?

  • The checkup. After every check, a short list of notes: what to fix soon, what is worth a look, what is good to know, and under them the checks that passed. Each note says what it is, why it matters and what to do, a step at a time, in the words your router’s own settings use. It looks at the doors your router opens to the internet, push-button pairing (WPS), how your Wi-Fi is protected, risky doors left open on devices, new devices, devices that stopped answering, a router that changed or something answering as it, and Perimeter itself. A check passes only if it ran in the last day, so “couldn’t look” never reads as “nothing there”. It can also be printed in the Terminal.
  • Answering it. “It’s mine” and “I don’t know it” for a new device (the second becomes a short plan), “Got it” for anything you expect, which moves to the checks that passed as something you said is expected, and “Give it a name”. A device answering as the router can be taken as the new router, asked once more first.
  • Names people recognise. Your name for a device first; then the router’s own list of names, which is how phones that announce nothing get one; tidied of serial numbers and tags; the maker as people say it (“Xiaomi”, not its legal name); and a best guess, with a sentence on what it probably is, where there was “Unnamed device”.
  • Two sheets. The checkup opens first; the devices are one swipe, click or switch away, in groups (phones and tablets, computers, TV and sound, smart home, printers, router and Wi-Fi, not sure yet), with look-alikes folded into one row, so eight bulbs are one line, and long groups shortened to “Show 7 more”. The Terminal has a picture of the same screens, with the same titles.
  • The iPhone tells you what the Mac announced, once, when it next asks; finds the Mac again when the router gives it a new address; says plainly when you are away from home or the Mac is asleep; and shows the last answer with its age until the Mac answers.
  • The Mac offers “Watch this network”, “Start it” when Perimeter has stopped, “Restart checks” when the checks have, and “Open at login”; its notifications come from Perimeter; it checks again the moment it wakes.
  • A Mac that sleeps. Perimeter writes down when the Mac slept. A gap a sleep explains is said as that, not as “Perimeter stopped checking”; a device a check missed while the Mac slept in it is “not checked”, not “away”; and no notification is sent on the strength of such a check. When the Mac slept through most of the week, a good-to-know note says Perimeter only watched while it was awake.
  • The dashboard shows the two sheets side by side on a wide screen and under a switch on a narrow one, works from the keyboard, keeps your place through a refresh, says “Check now”, and says how to start Perimeter again when it has stopped.
  • The words. Every sentence was read as someone who is not technical would read it, and fixed on every screen and in the Terminal: one word, “check”, for what Perimeter does; plain words first and the technical one in brackets after; steps that say “choose” and point at the button below; dates with the day first.

Before 0.4

The first screens

0.3 (28 September 2026) kept the week an hour at a time, on every screen and every device, and said when an always-on device went quiet. 0.2 (23 September 2026) added the screens: the dashboard, the Mac’s menu bar and the iPhone.
